UK Train Rides

A BI dashboard built for a National Rail scenario, exploring route popularity, peak travel windows, revenue by ticket type, and what drives on-time performance.

DatasetMock National Rail ticket data, Jan–Apr 2024
Scale19 variables per transaction
ToolsPower BI
RoleBI Developer

The Objective

Acting as a BI Developer for National Rail, the brief was to build a single dashboard answering four operational questions: which routes are most popular, when does peak travel happen, what revenue comes from different ticket types and classes, and what factors affect on-time performance.

Approach

Using mock ticket transaction data spanning January to April 2024 (19 variables covering transaction, passenger, station, and journey-outcome details), I selected the relevant fields, cleaned the data, engineered additional analytical columns, and modeled everything in Power BI to answer each of the four questions directly.

Liverpool–Manchester, London–Birmingham, and London–Reading were the busiest routes, with traffic concentrated in the morning (6–10am) and evening (4–8pm) commuter windows. Advance tickets outperformed Off-Peak and Anytime fares on revenue generated.

  • Introduce dynamic pricing around the identified peak windows.
  • Investigate the sales anomaly seen on the first day of each month.
  • Incentivize advance bookings further, given their outsized revenue contribution.
  • Add peak-hour capacity and invest in infrastructure to reduce signal-failure and technical delays behind the 13% of journeys that ran late.

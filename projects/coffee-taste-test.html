The Great American Coffee Taste Test

A data-driven market-entry strategy report for investors looking to open a coffee business in the U.S. — built around a real nationwide taste-test survey.

DatasetThe Great American Coffee Taste Test (2023)
SourceCometeer & James Hoffmann, ~5,000 kits
ToolsPower BI
RoleData Analyst

The Objective

Investors considering entry into the U.S. coffee market needed data-backed guidance on three questions: who to target, what to serve, and how to price it. This project used survey responses from "The Great American Coffee Taste Test" — a 2023 initiative by Cometeer and James Hoffmann that shipped roughly 5,000 coffee-tasting kits nationwide — to answer them.

Approach

I selected the variables relevant to the three focus areas — audience targeting, product analysis, and pricing strategy — cleaned the dataset to remove null values, and built a set of Power BI visuals covering demographics, consumption habits, taste preferences, and spend.

The core audience skews toward full-time employed adults aged 25–44, mostly drinking 1–2 cups of full-caffeine coffee a day. Pour-over, latte, and regular drip were the top drink preferences, and a lower-bitterness, higher-acidity blend ("Coffee D" in the study) ranked highest in taste tests.

  • Target the 25–44 demographic first, rather than a broad general audience.
  • Offer light-to-medium roast beans matching the preference for lower bitterness, higher acidity.
  • Serve black coffee with optional add-ins (granulated sugar, whole milk) rather than a heavily flavored menu.
  • Price basic cups at $6–8 — high enough to sustain margins, low enough to avoid the value-perception drop-off seen above $10.
